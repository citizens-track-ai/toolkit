## So You Want to Organise a Community Assembly

> “A community assembly is a structured, inclusive space where people come together to discuss issues affecting their communities, learn from one another, and develop practical actions to create positive impact.
>
> Unlike a typical public meeting, a community assembly is designed to:
>
> - **Bring together a diversity of perspectives and lived experiences**
> - **Create space for respectful dialogue and shared learning**
> - **Include people who are often excluded from decision-making**
> - **Turn deliberation into robust, concrete action**
>
> Community assemblies are grounded in the belief that communities already hold valuable knowledge about the challenges they face. By combining lived experience with shared reflection and practical planning, assemblies can generate solutions that are more relevant, trusted, and effective.”
>
> *— Source: [Assemblis Community Assembly Guide](https://assemblis.org/en/guide/)*

## Why Community Assemblies on Artificial Intelligence?

The **Community Assemblies on AI Toolkit** provides a set of open, adaptable resources to scaffold deliberative sessions addressing artificial intelligence (AI). 

We start from three foundational assumptions:

1. **AI is a vast topic.** There are many different issues that communities want to talk about—from everyday workplace automation and school guidelines to energy-hungry data centres and global safety treaties.
2. **Many people do not feel informed about AI.** There is an urgent need for grounded, non-technical learning and community capacity building.
3. **Everyone brings valuable experience and insights.** Regardless of background or technical literacy, every person experiences the societal impacts of AI and holds legitimate views on how technology should serve their community.

## Connecting Local Deliberation to Global Governance

The Community Assemblies on AI toolkit has a particular focus on helping communities identify **both local actions and key messages to be shared with global decision makers**.

As part of the **Public AI Voice and Engagement (PAVE) Data Commons**, this helps create inclusive evidence and deeper understanding of how artificial intelligence is affecting diverse communities across the world.

### About the PAVE Data Commons

**PAVE** stands for **Public AI Voice and Engagement**. The PAVE Data Commons is an open, global repository that documents a wide range of participatory processes gathering community voices on AI.

If your assembly outcomes and recommendations are recorded in the PAVE Data Commons, they can:

- **Feature on the PAVE Global Map of Engagement** shown to decision-makers and delegates at key AI governance summits around the world (including the UN Global Dialogue and Geneva AI Summit);
- **Feed directly into policy briefs, consultation responses, and reports** highlighting grassroots public priorities for the future of AI;
- **Form part of the evidence and advocacy work** of the Citizens Track on AI.

> [!NOTE]
> **The more detail you can provide on your community assembly, its outputs, and outcomes, the more we can help amplify your community's messages.**
