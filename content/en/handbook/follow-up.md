An assembly concludes with deliberation, but its community impact is only beginning. Stage 3 guides you through documenting outputs, sharing your data, and linking your community's voice to global policy.

---

## Post-Assembly Checklist

Work through this checklist within 14 days of completing your assembly:

- [ ] **Digitise Notes & Cards:** Transcribe small-group sticky notes, worksheet responses, and notetaker write-ups into a central document.
- [ ] **Collate Priority Actions:** Summarise the top 3–5 recommendations or community actions agreed upon by participants.
- [ ] **Send Participant Follow-up:** Email or mail attendees a short summary thanking them and confirming next steps.
- [ ] **Upload Assembly Report:** Submit your findings to the [PAVE Data Commons](https://assemblis.org) to ensure your community features on the global engagement map.
- [ ] **Engage Local Decision-Makers:** Send copies of your assembly declaration or recommendations to local councillors, community leaders, or regional representatives.
- [ ] **Submit to Target Consultations:** Transmit your recommendations to active multilateral consultation processes outlined below.

## Three Deliberation Domains

Assembly discussions typically address three interconnected domains:

### 1. Values and Vision
- *How do we live good lives alongside AI?* Formulating principles that prioritize human flourishing, dignified labour, and community well-being over raw automation.
- *What values should be embedded in systems?* Exploring whether publics prefer monolithic global AI models representing a single worldview, or diverse, culturally localized systems.
- *How can AI deliver sustainable public good?* Setting community priorities for public research funding and demanding moratoria on harmful uses.

### 2. Governance and Regulation
- *What global rules should govern AI?* Recommending binding international standards, red lines, and liability rules for frontier technology corporations.
- *What democratic control should local communities have?* Establishing community consent mechanisms for local data centres, surveillance cameras, and public sector algorithms.
- *AI safety assessments &amp; red lines:* Formulating public criteria for AI Safety Institutes and strict prohibitions on Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS).

### 3. Applications and Implementation
- *Public interest guidelines for high-stakes domains:* Clear boundaries for AI adoption in healthcare diagnosis, criminal justice sentencing, welfare allocation, and primary education.
- *Democratic decision-making:* Establishing rules for how AI tools can (and cannot) be used in political campaigns, public consultations, and civic debates.
