Deliberative community assemblies must maintain complete independence and community trust. When sourcing funds or sponsorship to support your event, adhere to the following six principles:

---

## 1. Free and Open Attendance

**No participant should ever be required to pay a fee or purchase a ticket to attend a Community Assembly on AI.** 

Assemblies are civic, deliberative spaces designed to include everyone, especially those facing economic barriers. Charging admission excludes critical community perspectives and fundamentally undermines the inclusive legitimacy of the assembly.

---

## 2. Transparent Voluntary Donations

You may include an **optional donation** element within your event registration form, or provide a collection bucket/card reader at the close of an in-person session, provided that:

- You are fully transparent about which legal entity will receive the funds and how they will be spent;
- It is made explicitly clear that donations are 100% voluntary and will not affect registration, seating, or participation in any way;
- You do not give the impression that donations are being made directly to the "Citizens’ Track on AI" unless you have prior written permission.

---

## 3. Local Funder and Business Support

Organisers are welcome to approach local funders, community foundations, municipal councils, and local businesses for cash sponsorship or in-kind donations (e.g., donated venue space, printing services, or catering).

- **Full Disclosure:** When submitting your assembly report to the PAVE Data Commons, you must list all funders and in-kind sponsors who supported your event.
- **Sponsorship Visibility:** Sponsors may be acknowledged on welcome slides or event agendas, but must not be given editorial control over discussion questions, deliberation materials, or assembly findings.

---

## 4. Preserving Assembly Independence

**Do not accept funding or in-kind support that could compromise—or appear to compromise—the neutrality and independence of your assembly.**

For example, an assembly focusing on the environmental impact of local AI data centres should not accept funding from a data centre developer currently seeking local planning permission. 

> [!CAUTION]
> If you are ever unsure whether a prospective funding source or partnership creates a conflict of interest, contact the CTAI coordination team for advice: [contact@citizens-track.org](mailto:contact@citizens-track.org).

---

## 5. Coordinating Regional Funding & Series Grants

If your group intends to approach local, regional, or philanthropic donors for larger funding to deliver a **series of community assemblies**, please contact us in advance at [contact@citizens-track.org](mailto:contact@citizens-track.org).

Early coordination allows the Citizens' Track on AI team to:

- Provide supporting letters of endorsement;
- Share template budgets and facilitator fee schedules;
- Avoid accidental duplicate funding applications to the same philanthropic foundations.

---

## 6. Accessing the $600 CTAI Micro-Grant

The Citizens' Track on AI provides **micro-grants of $600 USD** to support registered community assemblies. These grants are intended to remove financial barriers to delivery by covering:

- Accessible venue rental fees;
- Healthy food, lunch, and refreshments;
- High-quality full-colour printing for worksheets and deliberative cards;
- Local travel reimbursements or childcare assistance for participants.

To apply for a micro-grant, complete the [Community Assembly Registration Form](/handbook/register/) and indicate your funding requirements in the registration checklist.
