## Who controls AI?

Many different people have a role in determining the impacts that artificial intelligence might have in the world.

Think about the hopes and fears we have explored.

Who can act to address these?


## Private Industry & Technology Sector

* **'Frontier' AI firms** like Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta build and deploy AI models used in many products and services.

  * There are also a growing number of 'open source' or 'sovereign' AI models, built by governments, research institutions and public AI collaborations.  

* **Large companies** may build AI into their products or services, or provide AI services to the public sector.

* **Local firms** may have choices over what AI tools they use, how much they use it, and how they respond to potential harms.

Faciltitator guidance: 

* Identify examples of firms in your country or context that integrate AI. A very large scale example might be Microsoft, who have made choices about how AI is integrated into tools like Office 365. A more local example could be a retail or utility firm who have replaced call centres with chat bots. 

## Government and Public Sector

* **Global and regional institutions** like the United Nations, European Union or African Union can play a role in regulation of AI. 
  * The global governance of AI is still evolving and is mainly based on coordinating **voluntary committments**
    * The UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance (2026 and 2027) brings together all countries of the world to discuss AI issues.
    * The AI Summit Series, hosted by Switzerland in Geneva in 2027, brings companies, civil society and governments together to focus on 'Trustworthy AI for All' 

* **National governments** can set strategies, rules and regulations for AI. 

* **Local governments** make choices about how they use AI in service delivery, or how they promote access to and use of AI. 

## Public sphere

* **Representatives** speak on behalf of their constituencies and have influence.
  * Political representatives (diplomats, Ministers etc.) speak on behalf of their countries in international negotiations.
  * Media figures can influence public debate.
  * Civil society leaders advocate for public interests.

* **Associations** bring people together for collective influence. 
  * Community organisations and trade unions provide services and speak on behalf of their members. 
  * Faith groups can support their members to engage with a changing world.

* **Individuals and communities** make chocies about how to use, refuse and respond to AI. 




## Brainstorm pathway - ACTIVITY GUIDANCE

Ask groups to brainstorm all the different people they think can affect the issues they discussed earlier.

* This could be specific people or examples of groups.
* E.g. If your group wanted to see a slow down or pause in the development of AI so that society can catch up with its effects, you might name:
  * AI firms who can choose to slow development (or mention specific firms like OpenAI)
  * AI users who can choose whether or not to make use of the newest models - reducing demand 
* Encourage participants to name as many different people as they can, without judgement.

You can run the brainstorm:

* As a whole group brainstorm; or
* At small-group tables, with a facilitator and note-taker to record; or
* As a silent brainstorm onto post-it notes, and then asking participants to  cluster these onto the a shared board or wall. 

Introduce the matrix of AI control (see slide 2) and ask participants to consider whether there are any additional power holders they would add. 

(Optionally) Prepare a copy of the matrix to show on the wall with large paper, or post-it notes and masking tape. Map post-it notes from the brainstorm to these categories.

Outline that:

* When we think of actions, we should think about who needs to take that action;

* Think about where we have most control:
  * We might not have a direct line to influence AI firms... but we might be able to influence our representatives... who influence global bodies... who influence AI firms. 
  * Or we might not see a path to create change on our own, but as part of wider associations and collective action we might be able to better secure the opportunities of AI. 
  * We might not have control of how firms design AI tools, but we *might* have more control of the AI tools we choose to use. 

* Think about who can help: 
  * We can ask power-holders to change something
  * We can ask for support to create the change we want to see
  * We can offer our support and solidarity to others

Facilitator note: The goal here is not to ignore the reality of power imbalance in shaping AI. You might also highlight that these are not always easy paths to take - and that often the most powerful change comes from collective action.
