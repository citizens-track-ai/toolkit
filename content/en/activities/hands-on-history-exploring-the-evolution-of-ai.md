## Purpose

**The content for this session is based on:**

- Explaining the history of AI 
- Introducing different eras of AI development, and using this to highlight different kinds of AI
- Highlighting how AI development has not been a linear path: and that its future development and adoption is the product of choices made at different levels
- Making connections between AI and data governance
- Briefly introducing topics such as open source AI, local models and agentic AI

**The optional hands-on activities**:

* Allow participants with no experience of AI tools to get some hands-on exposure to AI tools (particularly to generative AI)
* Allow users with basic exposure to AI to explore different AI tools; 
* Support critical discussion of the pros and cons of AI use.

## Prerequisites

This activity is made up of three optional elements:

* **Presentation/quiz** — can be delivered with or without a screen/projector
* **Live demo** — internet access & AI tools/licenses required; can be delivered with or without a screen/projector.
* **Hands-on activities** — requires internet access and devices (we recommend at least one device for every 4 - 5 participants)

## Preparation

To facilitate discussions based on this activity, consider reading the Wikipedia page on [History of Artificial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_artificial_intelligence) or trialing the hands-on activities yourself to develop a deeper understanding of the history of AI.

## Process & Steps

1. **Introduction & Quiz (10-15 mins)**: Use the History of AI quiz or slide deck to test assumptions about when AI technologies emerged.
2. **Key Concepts & Timelines (10 mins)**: Highlight the Dartmouth 1956 workshop and contrast symbolic AI with modern generative models.
3. **Hands-on Exploration (15-20 mins)**: Participants test generative tools in small groups and reflect on choices, biases, and data sources.
4. **Group Discussion (10 mins)**: Connect AI choices back to democratic governance and collective decision making.

## Localisation & Adaptation Notes

* Research if there are any national or local milestones in computing/AI history relevant to your participants.
* If internet access is limited, the session can be run using the printable two-sided cards and offline paper exercises.
