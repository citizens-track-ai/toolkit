## Purpose

Scanarix AI Agora is a serious game on AI, media and information literacy and democratic deliberation. Players first investigate today's information arena, then deliberate as citizens on how AI should be governed. The activity should:

\- give everyone the same knowledge and vocabulary about AI, whatever they knew before, so the group can deliberate as equals;

\- practice concrete skills: telling claims from evidence, spotting AI-generated text and images and AI "hallucinations", noticing who gains from a message, and knowing when you have the right to be told that AI is involved;

\- turn those skills into shared civic questions: rights, responsibility, participation and action;

\- produce recommendations for educators, developers and policymakers, and record where the group still disagrees.

## Prerequisites

\- Up to 20 players per board, individually or in teams. Ages 16+.

\- No prior knowledge of AI is needed: the first part of the game builds a shared vocabulary. - Time: about 75 minutes (short version) or 2–2.5 hours (full game).

- A facilitator is optional. Without one, a player acts as host: they keep time, read out the steps, reveal the answer key and look after the Group Record.
- Room: a table large enough for the 50 × 50 cm board (an A4 board is also included), and space for groups of 4–6 to sit together in Part 3.

## Preparation

1\. Print the board, the cards and one Group Record per group (last page of the Rules & facilitator guide). Activity and Agora Cards are double-sided; all cards are 15 × 10 cm. 

2\. Get one token per player or team, a die, and up to 3 small tokens or coins per player for the "Who should fix it?" map.

 3\. Read the Rules & facilitator guide and the answer key.

 4\. Choose the full game or the short version. 

5\. Set up: put the board in the middle, shuffle the Activity Cards into a pile, sort the Key Cards by number, shuffle the Hint Cards into one pile, place the Tool and Lens Cards next to the board, and keep the Agora Cards aside for Part 3.

## Process & Steps

**Part 1. The Investigation (about 40 min)**

1\. Players take turns rolling the die and moving around the board. Each player starts with one Activity Card and takes another each time they land on an Activity space.

2. Each Activity Card shows a post, chat, advert, notice or image on the front and a question on the back. The player chooses one of the two Key Cards with the same number (blue or green). Only one is correct.

3\. Hint Cards, based on research and EU law, help players decide. After getting a Hint Card, a player may change their answer once. On a Lens space, a player takes a Lens Card, answers its question about one of their Activity Cards and keeps it to ask the group in the Agora.

4\. Part 1 ends when the first player completes a full lap, or when time runs out.

**Part 2. The Verdicts (about 15 min)**

5. Players present their answers, explain their reasons and say what they would do in real life: check it, report it, talk to someone or ignore it.

6\. Reveal the answer key and count points.

**Part 3. The Agora (45–60 min)**

7\.  Form groups of 4–6 (with 6 players or fewer, everyone is one group). Each group takes an Agora Card, ideally one linked to the Activity Cards it played (the links are on the front of each card). Players bring the Activity and Hint Cards they collected that fit the question as evidence. The facilitator gives each group the other Hint Cards listed on its Agora Card (without a facilitator, players take them themselves).

8\. First round: everyone shares a real experience connected to the question and says what they think, without interruption (1 min each).

9\. Perspectives: players turn the card over. Each player chooses a role, one from the card or their own, and argues it for one minute, even if they disagree with it.

10\. Lenses: players ask the Lens Card questions. Who gains and who pays? Whose voice is missing? Which rights are touched? What feeling does this want from us?

11\. Who should fix it? Each player places up to 3 tokens on the map in the centre of the board (Me, Community, Platform / company, State / law) and explains the choice.

12\. What can we do now? The group agrees on at least one thing people, communities or organisations can do without waiting for a law.

13\. Record: the group writes its recommendations for educators, developers and policymakers on the Group Record, checks support with fist to five (0 = I block it, 5 = full support) and writes down minority views.

**Timings Full game (about 2–2.5 hours):** getting started 10 min, Investigation 40 min, Verdicts 15 min, break and forming groups 5 min, Agora 50 min, optional sharing 30 min.

 **Short version (about 75 minutes):** Investigation 25 min, answers revealed without scoring 10 min, one Agora Card per group 40 min.

 Tips - Keep points light: the real result of the game is what the Agora produces. - When a group goes quiet, use the Lens Card questions.

 Ground rules for the Agora: listen to understand, not to win; challenge ideas, not people; changing your mind is a good result.

## Localisation & Adaptation Notes

Audiences and experience levels: the game is designed for players aged 16+ and adults, in schools, universities, libraries, community centres and civic education. Groups new to AI can start straight away: Part 1 builds a shared vocabulary, and Hint Cards 16–19 (what AI is, its risks and benefits) are useful for every case. For shorter sessions, use the short version.

Jurisdictions: the Hint Cards reflect EU law (AI Act, DSA, GDPR), the Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI and AI research as of 2026. Outside the EU, use the question on Hint Cards 37–38, "Is there a rule like this where you live?", to discuss local rules. The game is not legal advice; laws change quickly, so check the latest official sources.

Cultural contexts: all cases, people and brands are fictional and designed to work across different cultural and linguistic contexts. Some images and texts were created with AI and reviewed by the authors.
