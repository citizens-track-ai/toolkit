## Purpose

This icebreaker activity aims to:

* **Introduce a range of positive and negative impacts of AI.** 

* **Encourage participants to share their initial reactions to AI.**

* **Introduce critical thinking skills and explore different narratives around AI.** 

The first part of this activity can be run as a group are arriving for a session, or when the group are returning from a break. 

## Prerequisites

Before carrying out a group discussion of headlines, make sure you have established ground rules and working principles for the session.

## Preparation

This activity can take 2 - 6 hours to prepare for, as it involves finding and familiarising yourself with a range of news stories. You may also find it useful to [read up on 'AI Hype'](https://weandai.org/exploring-ai-hype/) and to do your own research on approaches for 'critically reading AI news stories'. 

* Get a set of 'emoji' stickers;
* Find _at least_ **five recent newspaper stories** about artificial intelligence;
* Take newspaper cuttings, or print the stories out on large paper, and place them on the wall in a collage - or print/photocopy a set of stories for each small groups, and give them to each group;

The stories should ideally represent a range of different perspectives on AI, and different AI issues. 

Look for stories that explore positive uses of AI, and stories that highlight risks or harms. Prefer stories with images, simple text and clear quotes to make the activity accessible for participants with low-literacy. 

If the session is looking at a particular topic such as AI in education, or AI data centres, you can choose stories that are broadly about AI and this issue. 

Try to find stories that represent different levels of decision making or that include different power-holders: local, national and foreign/international. 

## Process & Steps

**Part 1: Selecting**

* Invite participants to look at the different news stories. The lead facilitator asks _“What feeling do these headlines evoke in you?"_ Participants should stick the relevant Emoji onto the article.
* Once done participants take a seat and stay with the emotions for a couple of minutes.
* The lead facilitator explains that we may have many different reactions to AI: and that these are all welcome within the deliberation. They highlight that the stories show that experts do not all agree on the future of AI - and so there is an important role for communities to play in shaping the future. 

**Part 2: Sharing**

After introductions and ground rules for the session have been made, return to discuss the newspaper articles. 

* Ask participants to share a newspaper headline they had a reaction to and to share what it made them think about;
* Optionally, show the story on screen/on paper - and talk about the key points the story might raise. 

**Part 3: Deconstructing**

Ask small groups to pick a story each and read it in more depth asking:

* **What is the story about?** Is it describing a potential impact of AI? Or something that is already happening? What problem is AI creating or solving? Who benefits or is harmed? 
* **Who is behind the story?** Does the story originate from industry, civil society or government?
* **What evidence is presented?** Does the story make clear claims backed up with evidence from independent sources? 
* **Whose voices does it feature?** Who is quoted? Are the people affected by the story given a voice?

Invite groups to feedback from their discussions. Then the lead facilitator carries out a straw-poll on whether they feel the stories they have looked at tell the whole picture of how AI is working.
