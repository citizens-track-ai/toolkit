## Purpose

Each assembly should end by identifying **actions** that can be taken to address the opportunities and concerns that have been discussed. 

This activity starts by exploring who holds power to shape the future of AI, and then identifies potential actions. 

Actions can be:

* **Individual actions** that participants might take. (My actions)
* **Collective actions** that could be taken by the community. (Our actions)
* **Actions for others** to take. These can be expressed as general recommendations, or as 'asks' to specific power holders. (Their actions)

By the end of this session you should aim to have at least a prioritised list of **collective actions** and **actions for others** (recommendations or asks) that can be shared as part of your assembly report. 

Encouraging participants to identify **individual actions** can help to provide some immediate outcomes form the assembly, contribute to critical AI literacy, and support the group to develop their sense of agency with respect to AI. 

You can optionally document the individual actions that participants commit to. 

## Prerequisites

This activity should follow after a discussion of opportunities and concerns about AI, or a specific AI-related issue. 

## Preparation

* **Identify any specific power-holders, or opportunities for public voice, that you wish to focus on.** Adapt the agenda or slides to make sure you spend time explaining the particular opportunity, and to decide how much time to give to 'My actions', 'Our actions', 'Their actions'. 

* **Review the template Action Cards and create or adapt the cards to fit your context.** 

* **Print and cut out enough action cards for your group** or identify an alternative way to present actions (e.g. written out on large paper around the room). Make sure you include blank cards/paper for participants to add their own suggestions.

* **Decide on your prioritisation approach**. If you will be using 'dot voting' make sure you have coloured pens or sticky dots that can be used for this.
