## Purpose

This **Hopes and Fears exercise** helps any group talk openly about what they hope for, and fear, from artificial intelligence (AI). Each person writes down at least one hope and one fear, shares them with others, and the group looks for common themes and differences. 

It takes 30–60 minutes to run in full, and suits a group of 10–20 in one room.  If you have a larger group, run it at tables of 4–6.

There are no right or wrong answers, and you do not need to be an AI expert to run it. You can run this exercise via peer-to-peer facilitation, or with trained facilitators in one of these two modes:

- **Participants only.** People share in rotating pairs, then as a whole group.
- **With outside experts.** People talk in small groups, each with an AI researcher, scientist or policy maker, who shares their own hopes and fears alongside everyone else.

## Preparation

#### 
**Narrow the question.**

"Hopes and fears for AI" can be too broad a topic on it's own. 

Choose a more specific framing for your session and write it where everyone can see it. Either **use a time horizon** (already happening / next five years / distant future) **or a context** (AI in our community, or AI and work, health, school, public services).

#### **Prepare materials.** 

Two colours of post-it note, pens, a wall or board with two zones labelled Hopes and Fears, and a timer. 

Write HOPE or FEAR on each note, or label the zones in words, so colour is not the only signal. If you don’t have wall space the “zones” can also be created on large tables or on the ground.

#### **Selecting and briefing experts (expert version only).** 

Before the session, ask each expert to share their own hopes and fears rather than give a talk, to use plain words, to keep their turn the same length as everyone else's, and to listen as much as they speak. Where you can, choose experts who hold different views, and include people affected by AI or sceptical of it, so that no single perspective dominates a group.

## Process & Steps

This activity can be run in plenary with a small group (10 - 20 participants), or could be run at breakout tables with a larger group. 

* Ask the group to reflect on their **hopes** and **fears** for artificial intelligence
* Give participants two colours of post-it notes: one for hopes, and one for fears, and give them a few minutes to reflect and write-down at least one hope, and one fear

### With outside experts

* Split participants equally between the experts. Explain that _everyone_ has time to share their hopes and fears - not just the experts.
* The expert starts, and then go around everyone in turn, sharing hopes and fears written down on the post-it notes before. 
* After everyone has shared, open space for discussion. 
* If time allows, rotate experts between groups. 

### Participant-only version

* Participants form pairs and share their hopes and fears. As new themes emerge they write them down on post-it notes. 
* After a set interval (5 or 10 minutes) participants should switch pairs. 
* After a few rounds, bring the group back together to share their discussions. Collect post-it notes and cluster them around common themes. Pay attention to outliers as well.
